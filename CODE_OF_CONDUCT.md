# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Argue with the numbers, not with the person who brought them.

This project exists because people disagree about how to measure models. Sharp technical
disagreement is welcome and expected. Contempt for the people disagreeing is not.

## Our pledge

We as contributors and maintainers pledge to make participation in this project a
harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race,
religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

Many contributors here are students, and many are writing in their second language. Both are
normal and neither is a reason for anyone to be talked down to.

## Expected behaviour

- Assume the other person has a reason. Ask what it is before assuming they are wrong.
- Give evidence. "This number is wrong" is a start; "this number is wrong, here is the
  source, here is what it says" is a contribution.
- Accept that a decision can go against you after a fair argument. The weights are a
  judgement call, and reasonable people land in different places.
- Correct your own mistakes openly. Everyone here has shipped a wrong number.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal or political attacks.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments without new evidence, or dismissing
  contributions for their author rather than their content.
- Nationalistic point-scoring. This project deliberately ranks models from many countries in
  one table; "their models are cheating" without evidence is not analysis.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, discussions — and when someone is
representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report unacceptable behaviour by opening an issue marked private if the platform allows it, or
by contacting the maintainer through their GitHub profile.

Maintainers will review every report and respond in a way they judge proportionate. That may
mean a warning, having a comment removed, or being blocked from the repository. Maintainers
are obliged to keep the reporter's identity confidential.

Maintainers who do not uphold this code in good faith may face consequences decided by other
maintainers.
