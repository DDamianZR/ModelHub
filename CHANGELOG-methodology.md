# Methodology changelog

Every change to how a published number is computed raises `methodology_version` in
`config/weights.json`, and every entry here states the measured effect on the ranking rather
than the intention behind the change.

Composite values are **not comparable across versions**. Normalisation recentres the whole
scale, so the mean composite can move without any model getting better or worse. Only the
ordering carries across a version bump, which is why `models.json`, every row of
`history.jsonl` and every generated description records the version that produced it.

The effect figures come from `scripts/analysis/compare_rankings.py`, run over two snapshots of
`models.json` built from **identical source data**, so the difference is the formula and
nothing else.

---

## 1.1 — 2026-07-29

**Per-benchmark normalisation against a frozen reference.**

A raw score measures capability times benchmark difficulty. Averaging raw scores inside a
category therefore ranked models partly on which benchmarks happened to measure them:
FrontierMath has a median of 28.6 and LiveBench Mathematics 90.1, and both were averaged into
"math" as though they were the same measurement.

Each benchmark is now converted against a fixed distribution before its category average is
taken:

```
normalized = 50 + 12.5 × (raw − mean) / sd,   clipped to [0, 100]
```

The reference lives in `config/benchmark_reference.json` and is regenerated by
`scripts/analysis/build_reference.py`. LMArena stops being a special case and enters as one
more benchmark.

### Measured effect

| | |
|---|---:|
| models compared | 53 |
| composites that moved | 53 |
| mean displacement | 17.04 |
| max displacement | 31.56 |
| position changes | 48 |
| Spearman against the 1.0 order | 0.743 |
| mean composite before → after | 64.46 → 48.12 |

The mean falling by 16 points is the scale recentring on 50, not 53 models getting worse.

**Cohort dependence, which is what this was for.** Under 1.0, deleting `openai-gpt-5-nano`
moved the composite of 48 of the other 52 models by up to 8.03 and changed 33 positions, with
no benchmark value having changed at all. Under 1.1 the same deletion moves **0 of 52**.
`scripts/tests/test_normalize.py` pins it.

**Effective weights** moved most of the way to their stated values as a side effect, because
the gap was largely a scale artefact:

| category | stated | effective 1.0 | effective 1.1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| reasoning | 0.25 | 0.21 | 0.28 |
| coding | 0.25 | 0.20 | 0.30 |
| math | 0.20 | 0.21 | 0.19 |
| human preference | 0.15 | **0.29** | **0.12** |
| instruction following | 0.15 | **0.09** | **0.12** |

Maximum deviation fell from +0.14 to +0.05. The residual is published in
`data/weight_audit.json` and on `/methodology` rather than engineered away.

**MATH Level 5 stops scoring.** n=5 gives its standard deviation a 35% standard error and
every model sits between 94.9 and 98.1, so it does not discriminate either. Measured cost: 5
models move, by at most 2.64, and the one model that loses the category outright
(`alibaba-qwen3`) was already provisional. Under 1.0 the same exclusion would have moved 5
models by up to 15.08.

**Rejected alternatives, both on measurement.**

- *Percentile within the reference instead of z-score.* Orders models almost identically
  (Spearman 0.9965), so the choice costs no information — but it saturates where the
  interesting comparison lives: 23 category scores land above 95 or below 5 against 0 under
  z, because the normal CDF is flat at the extremes.
- *Normalising the category score to a fixed spread.* Would make stated and effective weights
  agree by construction, and would fabricate discrimination: `instruction_following` measures
  models that genuinely are alike (sd 5.6) and stretching it would make that likeness look as
  decisive as `math` (sd 11.7).

### Consequence for stored derivatives

All 47 editorial descriptions were written under 1.0 and say which of a model's categories are
relatively stronger. Normalising reordered those categories, and 39 of the 40 descriptions
carrying a comparison began contradicting the bar chart above them. Descriptions now carry the
version they were written under and the site withholds any that do not match the build.

## 1.0 — initial

Raw percentages averaged inside each category. LMArena's Bradley-Terry ratings min-max
normalised across the cohort present in each build, which is the defect 1.1 exists to remove.
